About rheumatologic conditions

Expert infusion care for complex inflammatory disease

Rheumatologic conditions — including rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, psoriatic arthritis, and ankylosing spondylitis — are chronic, often progressive diseases that require consistent, long-term management. Biologic therapies have transformed outcomes for many patients, offering disease control that was not possible with older medications.

These medications are administered by IV infusion on a scheduled basis — typically every 4 to 8 weeks — and require a clinical setting with trained nursing staff and monitoring capability. Our center is built specifically for this type of care.

"Consistent, expert administration of your biologic therapy is just as important as the prescription itself."

Frequently asked questions

Rheumatology infusion FAQs

Do I need a referral from my rheumatologist?+
Yes — all infusion therapies require a referral and prescription from your rheumatologist or treating physician. Your doctor will send the referral directly to us. We'll handle everything from that point — insurance verification, prior auth, scheduling, and medication ordering.
How long will my infusion take?+
It depends on your specific medication and dose. Orencia typically takes about 30 minutes, while Remicade and Krystexxa can take 2–3 hours or more. We'll give you a specific estimate before your first visit so you can plan your day accordingly. Bring something to do — a book, tablet, or headphones — and we'll keep you comfortable throughout.
What happens if I have a reaction during my infusion?+
Infusion reactions are uncommon but our team is fully trained to identify and manage them immediately. We slow or pause the infusion, administer appropriate treatment, and contact your physician. Many biologic infusions include pre-medications (antihistamines, acetaminophen, steroids) to reduce reaction risk. Your safety is our first priority at every visit.
Can I switch from hospital infusion to your center?+
Absolutely — and many patients make this transition because our center offers a more comfortable, personalized experience at a lower cost. All we need is a new referral from your rheumatologist and your current infusion records. We'll handle the rest, including transferring your prior authorization to our facility.

FAQs

Neurology infusion FAQs

Can you administer Tysabri here?+
Yes. We are a registered Tysabri TOUCH-certified infusion site. Tysabri (natalizumab) requires enrollment in the TOUCH Prescribing Program due to the risk of PML. We manage all program requirements including enrollment, JC virus antibody monitoring documentation, and infusion observation protocols.
How long does an Ocrevus infusion take?+
The initial Ocrevus infusions (two doses given 2 weeks apart) typically take 3.5 hours each, plus an additional 1-hour observation period after each infusion. Subsequent infusions may be shorter for eligible patients. We pre-medicate with methylprednisolone, an antihistamine, and acetaminophen before each infusion. Plan for a full day for your Ocrevus infusion — we'll make you as comfortable as possible.
Do you administer IVIG for CIDP?+
Yes — IVIG therapy for CIDP and other autoimmune neurological conditions is one of our most frequently administered therapies. Infusion duration varies based on your dose and the specific IVIG product prescribed (Gamunex, Privigen, Gammagard, etc.). Most CIDP patients receive IVIG every 3–4 weeks on an ongoing basis, and we work hard to keep your scheduling consistent and predictable.
Can you administer Leqembi for Alzheimer's?+
Yes. Leqembi (lecanemab) is administered every two weeks and requires ongoing MRI monitoring for ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities). We coordinate with your neurologist to ensure appropriate imaging is scheduled and results reviewed before continuing treatment. If you or a family member has been prescribed Leqembi, contact us to discuss the full treatment process.

Expert biologic care for Crohn's & colitis

Inflammatory bowel disease — including Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis — affects hundreds of thousands of patients in the US and often requires long-term biologic therapy to achieve and maintain remission. FAQs

Gastroenterology infusion FAQs

What is an induction series and how does it work?+
Many GI biologics — including Remicade and Entyvio — start with an induction series: multiple infusions given at weeks 0, 2, and 6 to establish therapeutic drug levels quickly. After induction, you transition to maintenance infusions every 8 weeks. We schedule and track all induction doses and transition you seamlessly to the maintenance phase.
What happens if I miss an infusion?+
Missing an infusion can increase your risk of a disease flare and may cause you to lose response to your biologic. If you need to reschedule, contact us as soon as possible — we'll work to get you back on track quickly. Your gastroenterologist will be notified of any schedule changes so they can advise on next steps if needed.
Can I eat normally before my infusion?+
Yes — you should eat normally before your GI infusion. A full meal beforehand is actually recommended. Unlike some procedures, there is no need to fast before an infusion. Staying well-hydrated is also helpful for IV access. If your physician has given you specific dietary instructions related to your condition, follow those.
Can I transfer my infusions from a hospital to your center?+
Yes, and many patients choose to do so for a better experience and lower cost. We just need your gastroenterologist to send a new referral for our facility, and we'll request your infusion history for continuity. Your prior authorization will need to be transferred to our site — we handle that process completely.

FAQs

Allergy & immunology infusion FAQs

How often do I need IVIG infusions?+
IVIG frequency for immunodeficiency patients is typically every 3–4 weeks, though your immunologist will determine the exact schedule based on your IgG levels, clinical status, and the product used. We work to establish a consistent, predictable schedule and contact you proactively to confirm each upcoming infusion.
Can I get Xolair injections at your center?+
Yes. Xolair (omalizumab) injections for severe allergic asthma and chronic urticaria are administered at our center. Xolair requires a mandatory 30-minute observation period after each injection due to the risk of delayed anaphylaxis. We have protocols in place to monitor and respond to any reaction.
How long does an IVIG infusion take?+
IVIG infusion time depends on your dose and the specific product. For most immunodeficiency patients, infusions take 2–5 hours. First-time infusions are always started more slowly to monitor tolerability, which may extend the time. Once we know you tolerate the infusion well, the rate can often be increased for subsequent visits.
Does insurance cover IVIG for immunodeficiency?+
IVIG for primary immunodeficiency is typically covered under the medical benefit (not pharmacy) and is generally well-covered by Medicare and most commercial insurance plans when prior authorization is obtained. We handle prior authorization completely and will advise you of your estimated out-of-pocket cost before your first infusion.

About GLP-1 therapy

A proven, medically supervised path to sustainable weight loss

GLP-1 receptor agonists — including semaglutide and tirzepatide — represent a major breakthrough in the medical treatment of obesity and overweight. Clinical trials have shown 15–22% average body weight reduction with these medications, a result previously only achievable through bariatric surgery.

Unlike diet programs or non-prescription supplements, GLP-1 therapy works by targeting the underlying biology of appetite and metabolism. When combined with lifestyle guidance, these medications can produce meaningful, lasting results for patients who have struggled with weight despite their best efforts.

"GLP-1 therapy isn't a shortcut — it's a clinically proven medical treatment for a complex disease."

FAQs

GLP-1 weight management FAQs

Am I a good candidate for GLP-1 therapy?+
GLP-1 medications are FDA-approved for adults with a BMI ≥ 30, or BMI ≥ 27 with at least one weight-related condition such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or high cholesterol. They are not appropriate for patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2. During your initial consultation we'll review your full medical history to determine if GLP-1 therapy is right for you.
What are the common side effects?+
The most common side effects are gastrointestinal — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, or constipation — and are most common when starting the medication or increasing the dose. These typically improve as your body adjusts. Starting at a low dose and titrating slowly significantly reduces side effects. We'll provide specific guidance on managing any GI symptoms throughout your program.
How quickly will I see results?+
Most patients begin to notice appetite changes within the first 2–4 weeks. Meaningful weight loss typically becomes evident by months 2–3, with continued progress as the dose is increased to the therapeutic target. The full effect of these medications is typically reached after 20–24 weeks of treatment. Individual results vary depending on the medication, dose, diet, and activity level.
Does insurance cover GLP-1 medications for weight loss?+
Coverage varies significantly. Wegovy and Zepbound are specifically FDA-approved for chronic weight management and are covered by some commercial plans — though many plans still exclude weight loss medications. If you have type 2 diabetes, Ozempic and Mounjaro may be covered under your diabetes benefit. We verify your specific plan coverage at intake and help identify manufacturer savings programs — Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly both offer significant savings cards that can dramatically reduce your cost.
Will I gain the weight back if I stop the medication?+
Research shows that weight regain is common when GLP-1 medications are discontinued, similar to stopping other chronic disease medications. This reflects the chronic nature of obesity as a disease. Our program focuses on building sustainable lifestyle habits alongside medication so patients have the best possible foundation for long-term maintenance — whether they continue medication or eventually transition off of it.
