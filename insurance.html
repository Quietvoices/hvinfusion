After your infusion, we submit all claims to your insurance directly. If there are any claim issues or denials, our billing team handles the appeals process — you won't need to fight with your insurer.
Understanding prior authorization
Prior authorization (PA) is a requirement from your insurance company that a medication is medically necessary before they'll cover it. It's common for biologics — and we handle the entire process for you.
We submit the prior auth request with your physician's clinical documentation. You don't need to do anything.
Most authorizations are approved within 3–10 business days. We notify you and your doctor immediately when approved.
If your insurer denies the auth, we file an appeal with supporting clinical evidence. We do not give up easily.
We track auth renewal dates for ongoing treatment and initiate renewals proactively. Your treatment never lapses due to an expired auth.
Have questions about your prior auth? Call our team at (845) 848-3626 and we'll give you a status update on any active authorization.
Cost savings

Why infusion here costs
less than at a hospital

Ambulatory infusion centers — like ours — are consistently less expensive than hospital outpatient infusion departments for the same medications and the same care.

Lower facility fees
Hospitals charge a facility fee on top of the drug cost — sometimes thousands of dollars per visit. As a freestanding ambulatory center we do not charge hospital facility fees, which means lower bills for you and your insurer.
Insurer-preferred setting
Many insurance plans now direct members to ambulatory infusion centers for biologic therapy — because it costs them less too. This means better coverage rates and lower cost-sharing for you in many cases.
Manufacturer assistance
Most biologic manufacturers offer co-pay assistance or patient support programs that can dramatically reduce your out-of-pocket cost — sometimes to $0. We identify and apply for these programs on your behalf.

Insurance FAQs

Common billing & insurance questions

How do I know if my insurance covers infusion at your center? +
The best way is to contact us directly — either by calling (845) 848-3626 or submitting our insurance verification form below. Our billing team will contact your insurance company on your behalf and provide you with a clear summary of your benefits within one business day. There is no cost or commitment required to verify your coverage.
What is prior authorization and how long does it take? +
Prior authorization is an approval from your insurance company confirming that a medication is medically necessary and covered under your plan. It's required for most biologic medications. We handle the entire prior auth process for you — submitting the request, providing clinical documentation from your physician, and following up with your insurer. Most authorizations are approved within 3–10 business days, though some plans may take longer. We will keep you informed throughout.
Will I owe anything out of pocket? +
Your out-of-pocket cost depends on your specific insurance plan — your deductible, co-pay, and co-insurance amounts. We'll give you a clear estimate before your first visit. In many cases, manufacturer co-pay assistance programs can dramatically reduce or eliminate your out-of-pocket cost. We apply for these programs on your behalf at no charge. We will never start treatment without making sure you understand your financial responsibility first.
My insurance wasn't listed — can you still see me? +
Possibly, yes. We are actively credentialing with additional commercial carriers and may already be in-network with your plan. We are also able to work with some plans on an out-of-network basis. Please call us at (845) 848-3626 or submit your information below and we'll check your specific plan. Don't assume we can't see you before checking with us — you may be pleasantly surprised.
What if my prior authorization is denied? +
Prior auth denials happen, but they are often overturned on appeal. Our billing team will file a formal appeal with supporting clinical documentation from your physician. We advocate strongly on your behalf. If the appeal is unsuccessful, we will explore alternative options — including peer-to-peer review requests, exception requests, and manufacturer assistance programs — before giving up.
How does Medicare cover infusion therapy at your center? +
Medicare Part B covers most infusion drugs administered at ambulatory infusion centers when they are considered medically necessary. Generally, Medicare pays 80% of the approved amount after your Part B deductible is met. Your Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan typically covers the remaining 20%. If you have a Medicare Advantage plan, your coverage depends on your specific plan — we will verify this for you at no charge.
Can I use my HSA or FSA to pay for infusion costs? +
Yes. Health Savings Account (HSA) and Flexible Spending Account (FSA) funds can be used to pay for qualified medical expenses including infusion therapy co-pays, deductibles, and co-insurance. We accept HSA and FSA cards as a form of payment.
